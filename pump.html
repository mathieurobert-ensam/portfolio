Centrifugal Pump: CFD Design Validation

Designing a pump, then checking with CFD whether the numbers can be trusted before relying on them.

ContextEnergetics project, Arts et Métiers
Team2 students
My roleDesign and CFD simulations, with my partner
Year2025–2026
Objective

Design a centrifugal pump delivering a head of 12.5 m at a flow rate of 11.5 m³/h, and verify its performance with CFD.

Tools & methods
  • CAD of impeller and volute
  • STAR-CCM+ (CFD)
  • Mesh convergence study
  • k-ε, k-ω SST, Reynolds stress models

STEP 01

Can the mesh be trusted?

At a fixed flow rate, the base size of the mesh was varied to measure its influence on the head and the efficiency. A finer mesh gives more accurate results but a longer computing time: a base size of 0.02 m gave the best compromise.

STEP 02

Which turbulence model?

The same case was run with three turbulence models (k-ε, k-ω SST and Reynolds stress) to check that the conclusions did not depend on the choice of model.

STEP 03

Characteristic curves and flow analysis

The flow rate was swept to obtain the head H(Q) and the efficiency η(Q) curves, and the trends were explained with simple physical arguments:

  • the head falls as the flow rate rises, since the power supplied to the pump is fixed;
  • the efficiency follows a bell curve, with a maximum of 77%;
  • the velocity fields show turbulence in the impeller and high velocities in the narrowest section of the volute.

Results
  • Simulated head of 12.65 m at the design point, above the 12.5 m required.
  • Efficiency of about 75% at the operating point (77% at best efficiency).

What I learned: a simulation is only useful if its results are questioned: the mesh and the model must be checked before the numbers are used.
