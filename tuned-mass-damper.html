Tuned Mass Damper for Tall Buildings

Can a pendulum reduce how much a tower sways in the wind? Modeling, scale model and wind-tunnel tests.

ContextSupervised research project, preparatory classes
WhereLycée Victor Hugo, Besançon
FocusModeling and experiments
Year2022–2023
Objective

Tall buildings such as 432 Park Avenue in New York can sway by tens of centimeters in the wind, which disturbs their occupants. The goal was to quantify how much a tuned mass damper can reduce these oscillations.

Tools & methods
  • Equations of motion
  • Numerical simulation
  • Scale model, stiffness measurement
  • Wind tunnel
Up to 30 %reduction of the oscillations
2experimental campaigns
3configurations compared
STEP 01

Modeling the tower

Equations of motion of a tower without damper, with a tuned mass damper, and with a viscous damper, to compare the three configurations.

Mechanical model: tower and tuned mass damper
Simulated response of the tower
STEP 02

Building and characterizing a scale model

A scale model of a tower was built and its equivalent stiffness measured, so that the damper could be tuned to the tower's natural frequency.

Tower model and tuning masses
STEP 03

Testing in the wind tunnel

The model was tested in the school's wind tunnel. When the video-analysis software failed, I fixed a ruler to the tunnel window to read the amplitude of the oscillations directly.

Scale model in the wind tunnel
Damper and the ruler used to read the amplitude
Results
  • The tuned mass damper reduced the wind-induced oscillations of the model by up to 30%.

What I learned: model, simulate, experiment, then compare: and when a measurement tool fails, find a simpler one rather than give up the measurement.
