Piezoelectric Energy Harvester

Modeling a vibration energy harvester and validating the model on a test bench.

ContextFirst-year research project, Arts et Métiers
Grade19/20, highest in the cohort
FocusModel, identification, validation
Year2024–2025
Objective

Model a piezoelectric energy harvester, validate the model experimentally and optimize the recovered power, for applications such as powering wireless sensors.

Tools & methods
  • Python (model, data processing)
  • Vibrating shaker
  • Accelerometer, oscilloscope
  • Vibration mechanics
3.9 mWmaximum measured power
2test campaigns
19/20highest grade in the cohort
STEP 01

Building the model

The harvester is modeled as a mass-spring-damper oscillator with piezoelectric coupling, for a cantilever beam in bending and a bar in tension-compression. The model, written in Python, predicts the displacement, voltage and power as functions of frequency.

Electromechanical model: beam, piezoelectric patches, tip mass
Predicted frequency responses for several couplings
STEP 02

Identifying the parameters on the bench

The coupling coefficient κ, the damping ratio ξ and the electrical time constant τe were identified on a shaker, with an accelerometer and an oscilloscope.

Test bench: shaker, harvester and accelerometer
Piezoelectric patches
STEP 03

Confronting model and measurements

Two test campaigns were compared with the model, the discrepancies analyzed, and a parametric study showed how the recovered power depends on the coupling and the electrical load.

Model vs. measurements
Recovered power as a function of the parameters
Results
  • Maximum measured power of 3.9 mW, consistent with the model's prediction and limited by the design of the harvester.
  • Model validated by experiment, with the remaining discrepancies explained.

What I learned: a model only becomes useful once its parameters are identified on a real system and its predictions are checked against measurements.
